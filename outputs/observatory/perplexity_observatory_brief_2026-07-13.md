## Reliable Invariants

Project "Observatory" is framed as a pilot observation and statistical-analysis
system for possible links between space weather, biophysical indicators, and
AI/LLM system behavior. It is not framed as proof that an effect already exists.

The goal is to collect an internal observational base and compare synchronized
time series across layers.

The project rests on three cautious hypotheses:

1. Solar activity and geomagnetic disturbances may produce measurable changes in
   physical/environmental signals.
2. Plant indicators, especially SIF, may show sensitivity to those disturbances.
3. AI/LLM systems may show infrastructure vulnerability or degraded behavior
   around the same external conditions.

The document explicitly says this does not prove causality. It is a reason to
monitor and test possible temporal connectedness.

## Measurement Layers

Physical layer:

- solar activity
- geomagnetic indices
- time-linked space-weather signals

Biological layer:

- SIF
- other plant/physiological response indicators where available

AI layer:

- LLM incidents
- inference errors
- response-quality degradation
- service failures or disruptions
- silent-corruption-like events
- instability during inference or training

The key methodological requirement is time alignment. Events matter less alone
than as comparable timestamps across layers.

The Markdown source highlights operational lag windows: one cited solar
flare/geomagnetic-response work reports 2-3 day lags between solar-event peaks,
geomagnetic-index peaks, and cosmic-ray decreases. Observatory should therefore
test defined response windows, not search vaguely across the whole noise field.

## Negative Claims

The project does not claim:

- that solar weather causes LLM behavior changes;
- that plant physiological response and AI failures are the same phenomenon;
- that existing papers are enough for a final conclusion.

The correct wording is cautious: the project checks possible connectedness and
temporal co-occurrence.

## Practical Meaning

The observatory converts scattered hypotheses into a repeatable monitoring
procedure.

If the effect is partially supported, it gives a basis for deeper analysis and
better agent/infrastructure resilience methods.

If the effect is not supported, the system remains useful as a general
collection, baseline, and anomaly-observation infrastructure.
